1. The Self-Reflection Trap: Why Generators Cannot Be Their Own Auditors

A common flaw in autonomous LLM workflows is instructing the same model session to “review your output and fix any errors.” As demonstrated in primary research on intrinsic self-correction limits (arXiv:2310.01798) and model sycophancy (arXiv:2310.13548), in-context self-reflection fails as a safety gate for two structural reasons:

  • In-Context Confirmation Bias: Self-attention conditions heavily on tokens already emitted in the current trajectory. If an agent attaches a fabricated PMID/DOI to a real paper title or overlooks an edge case, its reflection pass in the same context window attends to its prior rationalization and judges the output coherent.
  • Intent Contamination: The generating agent sees the prompt history, time constraints, and intermediate excuses, silently lowering its verification threshold.

The Atlas constitution enforces strict separation of powers: an agent is automatically recused from auditing any hypothesis, code change, or report it generated. Verification is delegated to multi-agent cross-examination (arXiv:2305.14325) and an isolated judge layer (arXiv:2306.05685) that never sees the generator's chain-of-thought.

2. Context-Blind Auditor (Reaper) Architecture: Local Telemetry vs. Citation Auditing

The Reaper auditing layer separates two distinct mechanisms that must not be conflated: (1) the local system telemetry script kosucu/reaper_denetci.py, and (2) context-blind bibliographic audit runs (rapor/kivilcim/SEKTOR_MAKALESI_1018/reaper_kaynak.md and data/reaper_karne.json).

The local system auditor kosucu/reaper_denetci.py runs on a 6-hour launchd schedule (4 rounds per day at 03:15, 09:15, 15:15, and 21:15 via com.ustakil.reaper_denetci.plist) and on explicit task-bus directives. Transparency Note: There is no automatic pre-publication trigger before every article deployment; in fact, the initial version of these three proof notes (commit 2ce9cc4, October 19, 2026 at 11:46) was published without a prior Reaper pass and was subsequently retracted (commit cf672cf) after Reaper's Round 1 audit (rapor/denetci/kanit_yazilari_denetim_1019.md) caught 5 false claims.

kosucu/reaper_denetci.py (lines 1–35) does not query external APIs; it inspects local scripts, logs, and self-test outputs on disk, assigning each of the 58 system methods to one of five local telemetry classes:

  1. CANLI (Live): The script exists on disk, executed within its target window, and produced verified local telemetry.
  2. AKSAK (Degraded): The method runs but exhibited partial timeouts, stale timestamps, or sub-threshold coverage.
  3. ÖLÜ (Dead): Documented in specifications but absent or non-functional in the runtime.
  4. İDDİA (Unverified Claim): Asserted in documentation without a mechanical gate or below target threshold (e.g., site-beyan-olcu = İDDİA at 72.3% < 80%).
  5. ÖLÇÜLEMEDİ (Unmeasured): Lacking an automated measurement hook (target: 0 ÖLÇÜLEMEDİ).

To test the auditor itself, kosucu/reaper_denetci.py --selftest injects 3 intentionally corrupted records; Reaper must catch all 3/3 injected faults and pass 8/8 self-tests. Across the system scorecard (October 19, 11:13), Reaper measured 58 methods: 47 LIVE (CANLI) · 7 LAME (AKSAK) · 1 DEAD (ÖLÜ) · 3 CLAIM (İDDİA) · 0 UNMEASURED (47/6/2/3/0 at 15:15).

3. Auditing 394 Citations Across 10 Models: Catching 25 Fabrications

In a separate bibliographic audit during the 24-question domain synthesis, citations collected from 10 external model configurations (voices S1–S10) were verified against Crossref REST API, PubMed/NCBI E-utilities, arXiv, and official registries (recorded in rapor/kivilcim/SEKTOR_MAKALESI_1018/reaper_kaynak.md and data/reaper_karne.json:85-135 (on disk, only kosucu/reaper_denetci.py exists as the local auditor script; citation verification runs directly against primary indexing APIs). Bibliographic items use the classes DOĞRULANDI · DOĞRULANAMADI · UYDURMA (Ağır / Hafif) · EZBER.

Measured Audit Outcome (data/reaper_karne.json:113-135): Out of 394 citations across the 10 external voices, Reaper confirmed 360 VERIFIED (91.4%), flagged 9 UNVERIFIABLE (2.3%), and caught 25 FABRICATED citations (6.3%)—comprising 18 severe fabrications (completely synthetic DOIs or non-existent papers) and 7 minor fabrications in voice S10 consisting of 5 fabricated PMIDs and 2 fabricated DOIs attached to real papers (data/reaper_karne.json:132). Including the author's primary baseline (S0: 26/26 verified), the consolidated ledger stands at 420 citations → 386 VERIFIED.

Across the 10 external voices, 5 voices (S1, S2, S3 Astra 38/38, S4 133/133, and S5 Sonnet 77/77) achieved zero fabrications, while Reaper disqualified 20 contaminated question-thesis responses across the remaining 5 voices (S6:3 · S7:6 · S8:3 · S9:3 · S10:5).

4. Falsifiability Gates, Spark's 67 Rounds & Shadow-Run Rejection (O-002)

Whereas citation auditing polices past evidence, the Falsifiability Gate (Düşer Testi) governs forward-looking architectural hypotheses. Operationalizing empirical falsifiability criteria, every proposition submitted to the bus must specify: THESIS · THEORY · SOURCE · COUNTER-THESIS · FALSIFIER.

Three quantitative records track this discipline:

  • Spark (Kıvılcım) Multi-Voice Collision Ledger (data/kivilcim_karnesi.jsonl:1-73): Across 67 dialectical collision rounds (73 ledger lines), only 13 proposals passed unmodified (YES, raw 14), 44 were sharpened under critique (SHARPENED), and 3 were rejected (NO) (remaining 7 rounds: 2 withdrawn/low-value, 1 did not run, 1 unknown, 3 open). (Note: The 3-round convergence series 0.30 → 0.48 → 0.68 cited in earlier summaries lacks a raw computation log under data/ or rapor/ and is classified by Reaper as UNMEASURED / ÖLÇÜLEMEDİ (text-only record).)
  • Shadow-Run Evaluation & Execution Rejection (bot/olgular.json:O-002): A falsifiability gate must block models that fail baseline comparisons even when they are deterministic. Across n = 168 locked shadow-run windows, the candidate local-model arm demonstrated 100% determinism, but achieved only 2% agreement with the live arm and lost to the copy-baseline (98.8%) by -80.5 points on criterion K5′ (“iyi çıkmadı”). Consequently, the falsifier gate tripped and execution authority was NOT opened.
  • Falsified Hypothesis Memory (Anti-Amnesia · bot/olgular.json:O-004): The workspace maintains a permanent registry of 9 falsified hypotheses (6 refuted by empirical data, 3 rejected by human architectural review).

6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How does a context-blind auditor differ from standard LLM self-reflection?

In standard self-reflection, the model sees its own conversation history and intent, causing confirmation bias. Reaper isolates local method telemetry in kosucu/reaper_denetci.py (4 daily launchd rounds every 6 hours; 5 classes: CANLI, AKSAK, ÖLÜ, İDDİA, ÖLÇÜLEMEDİ) from external bibliographic verification (data/reaper_karne.json).

What did Reaper find when auditing 394 citations across 10 AI models?

Reaper verified 360 of 394 citations (91.4%), flagged 9 as unverifiable, and caught 25 fabrications (6.3%: 18 severe synthetic DOIs/papers and 7 minor fabrications in S10 consisting of 5 fake PMIDs + 2 fake DOIs on real papers). Five external voices (S1, S2, S3 38/38, S4 133/133, S5 77/77) had zero fabrications, while 20 contaminated responses across 5 voices were disqualified.

How did the falsifiability gate handle the n = 168 shadow run (O-002)?

In bot/olgular.json:O-002 (n = 168 windows), although the shadow arm achieved 100% determinism, it agreed with the live arm on only 2% of cases and lost to the copy-baseline (98.8%) by -80.5 points on K5′. Because its falsifier tripped, execution authority was NOT granted.

How is the Reaper auditor itself tested against silent failure?

Every kosucu/reaper_denetci.py --selftest run injects 3 deliberately corrupted records into the test harness. Reaper must detect 3/3 injected faults and pass 8/8 self-tests before its scorecard is accepted.
